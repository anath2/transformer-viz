        About

        Model: TransformerLens callummcdougall/attn_only_2L_half — 2 layers, 12 heads each, d_model 768, d_head 64, no MLP, no LayerNorm, shortformer positions (positions go into Q and K only). Material: ARENA 1.2 “Intro to Mech Interp”. Tokenizer: EleutherAI/gpt-neox-20b (Apache-2.0). The weights and the tokenizer are fixed. You control the prompt, the generation length, the heads, the ablations, the backend and the views.

        Private course site. All computation runs in your browser. No tokens, text or activations leave the page. This prototype uses deterministic mock activations with a planted induction structure. It does not use real weights.

        Off the rail

        You can click all heads. Pan and zoom freely. In this mode, a plain scroll zooms the map. The story stays paused at your last stop. The ablations and the prompt do not change.

          Design notes — Variant A2, “Story with doors”, 2D map

          Concept

          The scroll story is the frame of the page. On the left, there is one card for each stop. Each card has plain English, math in the notation of the paper (rendered with KaTeX) and colour-coded shape badges. On the right, there is a flat map of the model that you can zoom. The map uses the visual language of A Mathematical Framework for Transformer Circuits. Each stop frames the part of the model that it discusses. The important heads for the current step glow as doors. A click on a door opens its causally masked T×T attention pattern in place. The map then zooms until the full grid and its axis labels fit. A panel next to the grid, in the map column, shows the exact values, the cell breakdown and the TransformerLens equivalents.

          Story and exploration

          • On the rail: the document scrolls normally. A stop becomes active when its top edge goes above 40% of the window height. N/P, PgUp/PgDn and the chapter rail move between stops. Doors keep first-time students away from the 22 heads that are not important yet.
          • Off the rail (the button, E, or “Finished the exercises?”): an Explore panel replaces the story. The panel contains the prompt, generation, the all-heads table, the Q/K/V composition panels, and the link and loss tables. You can click all heads. The prompt, the ablations and the open grid do not change.
          • Back returns to the same stop and scroll position.
          • Novice path: read the story, open doors (each door shows a “look for” hint) and turn ablation on or off in the card. Expert path: set the prompt, step off the rail, sort the table, change the head fill, open heads with [/] and click cells to see q·k, vectors and hook names. “Math only” removes most of the prose.

          Map mapping

          • Vertical = depth, from bottom to top as in the paper: grey tokens → tan embed → residual stream (grey line with arrowheads) → layer 0 heads, which branch off a bus and join again at ⊕ (x₁) → layer 1, with the same layout (x₂) → tan unembed → grey logits. A dashed line shows that positions go only into the Q/K inputs (shortformer). Next to each row, behind a thin rule, grey text shows the equation for that row.
          • Heads are the dots from the composition figure. The previous-token head 0.7 is red. The induction heads 1.4 and 1.10 are teal. All other heads are grey. The fill level of a dot and the number below it show the selected quantity. Red hatching with ∅ shows a zero-ablated head.
          • Links are thin grey curves from each layer-0 head to each layer-1 head. The width of a link is proportional to its composition score. When “subtract baseline” is on, the map subtracts the 0.05 random baseline and hides links at or below it. Dominant links (more than 3× the baseline) use the colour of the input that they feed: K green, Q teal, V red. In step 4, the links 0.7 → 1.4/1.10 are animated. Links use only the weights, so ablation does not change them. When an ablation is active, the dock shows a note about this.
          • Induction colour code, used in all views (story, token strip, grids): key = green (B, with a dashed green outline on the earlier A), query = teal (second A), information that attention moves = red, logit effect = purple (predicted B).
          • Grid: the app draws the grid on a screen-space canvas that follows the map transform. Thus the grid stays sharp at all zoom levels. Lower triangle: grey sequential scale on √weight. Upper triangle: hatched mask. There are few axis labels (one in five at T=101). Role letters have their own lane, so they do not overlap the numbers. The grid opens in 220 ms (immediately with reduced motion).

          Tradeoffs

          • Doors help novices, but they add a mode switch for experts. One key and shared state make this switch easy.
          • Only one grid can be open at a time. To compare heads, move between them with ◀ ▶.
          • At T=101, a fitted grid cell is approximately 5 px. For precision, zoom in, hover, or use the keyboard cell cursor.
          • The equation column is part of the map. Thus, on the focused stops, the column moves out of view. A small pinned KaTeX label (top right of the map) keeps the equation of that row visible.
          • A framing does not cut a row of the diagram. The app adjusts the view of each stop so that each row (heads with labels, boxes) is fully in view or fully out of view.
          • On the rail, a plain wheel movement over the map scrolls the story. A hint shows one time: “To zoom, use Ctrl/⌘ + scroll or pinch”. To zoom, use Ctrl/⌘ + wheel, pinch, the buttons or the keys. Off the rail, a plain wheel movement zooms.
          • The head panel is in the map column next to the grid, so it does not cover the story text. The cost is a narrower grid (approximately 440 px at 1440×900).
          • The story claims use numbers from the clean run. When ablations are active, a red note below each claim gives the ablated values in words.

          Draft paragraph for “Interactions and UI”

          The app is a guided scroll story next to a 2D map of the model that you can zoom. The map uses the style of the architecture figure in A Mathematical Framework for Transformer Circuits. A vertical residual stream goes from the tokens and the embedding, through two rows of twelve attention heads, to the unembedding and the logits. The heads branch off the stream and join it again at ⊕. The equation for each row is next to that row. Heads are dots with a colour for each role (previous-token red, induction teal). The fill of a dot shows a selected quantity: the induction, previous-token or first-token score, or the loss increase from ablation. Curved links between the layers show Q-, K- or V-composition scores. These scores depend only on the weights. A baseline toggle hides links at chance level. Students pan and zoom with the wheel, pinch, drag, the buttons or the keys. Each story stop frames the part of the model that it discusses. At each of the six induction steps, the important heads glow as doors. A click on a door opens its causally masked T×T attention grid in place. The grid uses the induction colour code (key green, query teal, moved information red, logit effect purple). When you hover over a cell, the app shows its value and tokens. When you click a cell, the app shows the q·k/√d_head breakdown and the Q/K/V detail, with the TransformerLens equivalents. Zero-ablation updates the patterns, the scores and the per-token loss immediately. During greedy generation, the open grids become larger. “Step off the rail” makes all heads available, with tables as text equivalents.
